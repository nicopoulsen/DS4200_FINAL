1. Heatmap: Correlations Between Features


For our first visualization, we created a heatmap with Seaborn, which shows a visual representation of the correlation matrix between features and car price. It shows that mileage has the greatest impact on car price, with the strongest magnitude of correlation. Year is the second most significant feature, with the next greatest magnitude of correlation. Door count had the least impact on car price, with an almost 0 correlation coefficient. The strong correlation between Year and Price may reflect not only the age of the vehicle but also the integration of modern features and compliance with recent regulations. The negative correlation between Mileage and Price highlights the importance of vehicle usage in determining value, which could be further explored by examining how this relationship varies across different vehicle types or brands.






2. Scatter + KDE: Mileage vs Price


For our second visualization, we created a scatter plot showing the relationship between mileage and price with kernel density estimation to show areas of high density. This shows where the greatest concentration of cars falls on the mileage/price axis. It conveys the same strong price-mileage relationship, showing not only the obvious negative trend but also that the majority of vehicles are clustered around a central line, demonstrating the near-linear nature of this relationship. The concentration of data in the mid-mileage, lower-price region suggests a common depreciation pattern, which could be further analyzed to determine optimal mileage thresholds for resale.​ Outliers in the plot, such as high-mileage vehicles with relatively high prices, may represent well-maintained or rare models. Identifying these exceptions could provide insights into factors that mitigate depreciation. This visualization reinforces our earlier finding from the heatmap and emphasizes the importance of mileage as a resale predictor.




3. Lineplot: Brand Depreciation Over Time


This line chart shows the relationship between the year of manufacture and average vehicle price. It highlights a strong positive linear trend, indicating that newer cars tend to be more expensive. We decided to show this as a combined line across all brands as we noticed that pricing trends tend to be consistent industry-wide, regardless of make. The shaded region represents the confidence intervals, giving insight into the variability of pricing. This visualization is particularly useful for understanding how vehicle prices have evolved over the years and reinforces that year of manufacture is a strong predictor of average car price, which effectively helps answer our guiding question.




4. Altair Interactive: Explore Resale Prices by Year and Brand 


For our fourth visualization, we created an interactive Altair chart that visualizes how car prices vary over the years for multiple brands. The scatter plot allows users to explore brand-specific pricing patterns by plotting each vehicle's manufacturing year on the x-axis and its resale price on the y-axis. Each point represents an individual vehicle, making it easy to identify clusters of high- or low-priced models across time.


By using the brand dropdown, users can isolate specific manufacturers and compare how each brand's vehicles depreciate with age. The price slider below the plot provides additional control to focus on budget-friendly or high-end vehicles, helping to remove outliers or narrow in on a target price range. This makes it especially useful for consumers who want to identify good-value options or analysts seeking to understand brand-level depreciation trends.


The plot is zoomable and pan-enabled, letting users inspect specific regions more closely. Additionally, a Reset Zoom button is provided for convenience to return to the default view at any time. Interactive exploration can reveal how external factors, such as economic downturns or fuel price fluctuations, impact different brands differently.​ Comparing brands over time may uncover shifts in market positioning or consumer perception, providing context for pricing trends.​ This interactivity turns a traditional static plot into a flexible exploration tool that supports both high-level overviews and focused inspections.








5. D3 Interactive: Compare Average Prices Across Car Features


For our fifth visualization, we created interactive bar charts with D3.js, to analyze car prices based on various attributes. These charts dynamically display the average car prices grouped by selected features, such as brand, model, year, transmission type, fuel type, engine size, number of doors, or owner count. Users can filter the data by car condition (e.g., newer cars that are 5 years old or less, or older cars over 5 years old) and select a grouping feature through dropdown menus. The chart updates automatically when filters are applied or grouping features are changed, ensuring real-time visualization of insights based on user preferences.


The charts incorporate several interactive elements to enhance usability. Bars change color from steel blue to orange when hovered over, and a tooltip appears to display the exact average price for the hovered bar. The x-axis represents the selected feature's categories (e.g., brands or years), while the y-axis shows average price values in dollars. Numerical features like mileage or engine size are sorted numerically, whereas categorical features like brand are sorted by price values.


These visualizations allow users to identify trends such as which brands, models, or fuel types tend to have higher average prices. They also enable comparisons across groups—for instance, analyzing differences between newer and older cars or examining how average prices vary with attributes like transmission or engine size. Such insights can support decision-making related to car pricing strategies or purchasing preferences and help consumers understand which vehicle attributes have the greatest impact on resale price. According to the d3 bar charts, the features that had the most variation in average price were year, engine size, fuel type, and transmission.
